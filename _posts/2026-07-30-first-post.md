## Who: Anthony Reid

Hello all, welcome to my first blog! I should introduce myself. My name is Anthony. I am a Data Fusion Engineer at NAWCAD, and, currently, enrolled to be an Electrical and Computer Engineering PhD student at the University of Maryland. 

## What: My Research Interest

My research interest is to explore the following core research question: "How do we mathematically represent uncertain, multimodal, time-varying information so intelligent systems can make reliable classification, estimation, and decision-making?". 

## Why: What Drives My Research Interest

To explain myself further, I noticed that most research have gravitated towards using AI as this duct tape solution for almost everything. In relation to data fusion, it seems as if AI has been pushing away data fusion solution. The best example I could use is seeing the research of self-driving cars. It seems most focus on expanding just Computer Vision and Neural Networks, and training for the things expected, like pedestrians and signs. But, most end up ignoring prominent sensors like LIDAR in favor of more cameras for the 360 vision. This, in turn, leads to losing data from things that could effect the camera, like the weather.

The point is, I would like to explore non-AI solutions through data fusion that can enhance the solutions of intelligent systems when AI fails. And, look, I am not hating on AI, I have been an AI-advocate since college, and was fighting for more research into AI before the booming interest in AI happened around 2020. I'm just annoyed at how AI has been overingly abused, so I am choosing to focus on non-AI solutions while building up the field of data fusion. 

In addition, I chose to focus on mathematical models for two reason:

- I'm a (self-proclaimed) math masochist
- (More serious reason) I want my research to be agnostic to any application of data fusion

To expand on that second point, yes, I work in Defense. But data fusion is not limited to Defense. It has applications in biomechanics, robotics, finances, etc. I, myself, would like to personally explore applying data fusion to audio/music information retrieval, the application that got me into engineering in the first place. For me, making my research more agnostic allows me to stay viable to apply my skillsets anywhere I want to. By focusing on math, I would be strengthening how to translate the math models for any application I'd like to, while not being locked into one application. Admittedly, I am also inspired by the show Numb3rs, where the math professor applied math to solving crimes. As a kid, I always wanted to do something similar, but for any field I liked, such as music or robotics/autonomous systems.

So yeah, that's my story. I will continue to post when I feel like as I go through the program. Peace!
